# Design-partner terms: options and recommendation

*Not legal or tax advice. Before anything is signed, have the structure documented by a commercial lawyer and an accountant. That includes the IP assignment, the shareholder terms and the GST/tax treatment.*

## Starting point: make Practy a separate company

Whatever deal is chosen, **Practy should be its own company (e.g. Practy Pty Ltd), separate from the law practice.** Reasons:

- **Liability and insurance.** Software liability should stay off the firm's professional indemnity policy and the trust account.
- **Regulatory separation.** Soul Legal is the regulated law practice and the data **controller**. Practy is a technology vendor and data **processor** under a data processing agreement (DPA). This keeps Uniform Law, privilege and confidentiality responsibilities clear.
- **Selling and fundraising.** Other firms (including competitors) and investors need a standalone vendor with clean IP ownership.
- **Client data.** Soul Legal's client data never becomes Practy's property. It is processed under the DPA, is not used for model training, and can only be used in de-identified form with consent.

## The options

| # | Model | Soul Legal gets | Practy gets | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Founding customer** | Free pilot; then a locked discount (typically 30–50% off list for 2–3 years) and most-favoured pricing | Feedback, a reference customer, a case study | Simple; standard SaaS practice | No share in the upside, despite Soul Legal's IP and time making the product |
| **B** | **IP licence + revenue share** (like the A&O Shearman / Harvey arrangement) | As A, plus a royalty (typically 3–10% of revenue from the PI module, for a fixed term or to a cap) | A licence to Soul Legal's workflows, templates and know-how | Ties the reward to the IP contributed | Ongoing royalty accounting; investors dislike revenue claims on top-line revenue; can distort pricing |
| **C** | **Equity for design partnership** | As A, plus equity or options (typically 1–5%) vesting on milestones (pilot live, 3 clean reconciliations, second firm signed) | An IP assignment, a committed pilot, a reference customer, advisory input | Aligns everyone with long-term value; clean for investors; no royalty admin | Value is illiquid until an exit; needs a cap table and shareholder agreement |
| **D** | **Co-founder / joint venture** | A large stake (often 10–30%+) and board seat(s) | IP, the pilot, domain leadership, possibly funding | Maximum upside; deep commitment | Governance complexity; Soul Legal's attention split between practice and startup; harder to sell to competitor firms |

## Recommendation: **Option C (equity + founding-customer pricing)**, with these terms

1. **Pilot:** free during the pilot (about 12 weeks from go-live) and through the parallel trust-ledger run.
2. **Pricing:** founding-customer pricing afterwards, around 50% off list for 3 years, then most-favoured pricing for life.
3. **Equity:** options or shares in Practy Pty Ltd vesting on milestones rather than time:
   - pilot live on ICWA motor vehicle and WorkCover;
   - success measures met (hours saved, zero missed key dates, ≥95% filing accuracy);
   - trust ledger switched over after a clean examiner review;
   - second paying firm signed with a Soul Legal reference.
4. **IP:**
   - Soul Legal **assigns or licenses** its generic workflows and templates to Practy on a perpetual, worldwide basis. These include the Authority to Settle logic, particulars method, bill of costs method and naming convention.
   - **Excluded:** client data, the Soul Legal brand and letterhead, and anything identifying clients.
   - Practy owns the product. Soul Legal gets a perpetual right to keep using whatever was built from its contribution.
5. **Data:**
   - a DPA naming Soul Legal as controller;
   - Australian hosting;
   - no training on client data;
   - de-identified outcome analytics only with opt-in;
   - full export in open formats on exit.
6. **Commitments from Soul Legal:**
   - named champions (one principal, one fee earner, one admin/accounts person);
   - a fortnightly 1-hour feedback session;
   - a baseline timesheet sample;
   - permission to act as a reference and appear in a case study (de-identified).
7. **Conflict hygiene.** Kevin Wong and any other firm principals holding Practy equity should note that interest in the firm's records. The vendor agreement should be on arm's-length terms so the firm's duties to clients aren't compromised by its interest in the vendor. Client-facing AI-use disclosure goes into the costs agreement.

**Why C over B:**
- A royalty makes pricing worse for every future customer and puts off investors.
- Milestone equity rewards the same contribution and aligns Soul Legal with Practy's success beyond the pilot.

**Why C over D:**
- It keeps Soul Legal focused on running the practice.
- It keeps Practy neutral enough to sell to other PI firms, including Perth competitors.

**If Practy is being founded by Soul Legal's own principals,** C and D merge. The founders hold equity directly. What still matters is the separation:

- a separate company;
- an IP assignment from the firm to Practy;
- an arm's-length customer agreement and DPA between the firm and Practy;
- the same data and conflict safeguards as above.
